Tip #1: Build a Strong Foundation with Healthy Soil

Beautiful landscaping starts below the surface. Healthy soil gives grass, shrubs, flowers, and trees the nutrients they need to thrive during periods of high temperatures and changing weather conditions. While it is easy to focus on mowing or watering, soil quality often has a greater impact on the long-term health of your landscape.

Before making seasonal improvements, inspect areas where grass struggles to grow or where water tends to collect after storms. Compacted soil, poor drainage, or erosion can limit healthy root growth and reduce the effectiveness of watering and fertilizing.

Simple practices that help improve soil health include:

  • Adding quality topsoil where needed
  • Applying mulch around plants to retain moisture
  • Aerating compacted lawn areas
  • Reducing standing water through proper grading
  • Replacing nutrient-depleted soil in problem areas

Properties throughout Athens experience varying soil conditions, making it important to evaluate each landscape individually rather than relying on one solution for every yard.

For homeowners planning larger outdoor improvements, addressing soil conditions first creates a stronger foundation for future landscaping projects.

Tip #2: Smart Water Management Makes a Big Difference

Water plays one of the biggest roles in maintaining a healthy landscape during Alabama summers. While lawns require moisture to stay healthy, too much water can be just as damaging as too little.

Instead of watering every day, focus on watering deeply and less frequently. This encourages deeper root systems that are better equipped to handle extended periods of heat.

Consider these watering practices during Summer 2026:

  • Water early in the morning whenever possible
  • Avoid watering during the hottest part of the afternoon
  • Check irrigation systems regularly for leaks
  • Watch for areas where water consistently pools
  • Adjust watering schedules after rainfall

Drainage also deserves attention. If water consistently collects around your home or landscape beds, it may indicate grading issues rather than watering problems.

Professional grading can improve water movement across your property, helping protect lawns, flower beds, and structures from unnecessary water damage while supporting healthier plant growth.

Tip #3: Focus on Seasonal Maintenance Before Small Problems Grow

One of the easiest ways to keep your property looking its best is by staying ahead of routine maintenance. Small issues often become larger and more expensive when left unattended during the busy summer season.

A regular maintenance schedule helps preserve both the appearance and functionality of your outdoor spaces. Seasonal landscaping tasks may include:

Refresh Mulch Around Landscape Beds

Fresh mulch helps regulate soil temperature, reduces weed growth, and helps retain moisture during hot weather. It also provides a clean, finished appearance that complements plants and hardscape features.

Trim Trees and Shrubs Carefully

Pruning dead or damaged branches encourages healthier growth while improving safety around your property. Proper trimming also allows more sunlight and airflow to reach surrounding plants.

Keep Grass at an Appropriate Height

Avoid cutting grass too short during the summer. Slightly taller grass shades the soil, helps retain moisture, and encourages stronger root development.

Monitor Drainage Throughout the Season

Heavy summer storms can quickly expose drainage concerns. Identifying these issues early allows property owners to address them before erosion or standing water creates larger problems.

Tip #4: Plan Improvements That Add Long-Term Value

Summer is an excellent time to think beyond routine maintenance and consider improvements that will continue benefiting your property for years to come. Instead of waiting until problems become noticeable, homeowners can use the warmer months to evaluate how well their landscape supports everyday use, drainage, curb appeal, and future projects.

Walk around your property and look at it from different perspectives. Ask yourself questions such as:

  • Does water drain away from the home after heavy rain?
  • Are there uneven areas that make mowing difficult?
  • Have certain sections become overgrown?
  • Would additional planting beds improve curb appeal?
  • Is there enough usable outdoor space for your family's needs?

Answering these questions can help you prioritize projects based on function as well as appearance.

Tip #5: Protect Trees, Shrubs, and Lawn During Summer Heat

Even healthy landscapes can experience stress during Alabama's hottest months. Extended periods of heat combined with occasional heavy rainfall create conditions that require ongoing attention throughout the season.

Protecting your landscape is not always about doing more. In many cases, it comes down to following consistent maintenance practices and responding to changing weather conditions.

Water Deeply Instead of Frequently

Deep watering encourages stronger root systems that are better able to tolerate dry conditions. Frequent shallow watering often keeps roots close to the surface, making lawns and plants more vulnerable during periods of extreme heat.

Add Mulch Where Needed

A fresh layer of mulch helps retain moisture, regulate soil temperatures, and reduce weed growth. It also gives landscape beds a clean, finished appearance while supporting healthier plants throughout the summer.

Watch for Signs of Stress

Brown patches, curling leaves, thinning grass, and wilting plants can all indicate that your landscape needs attention. Identifying these signs early makes it easier to correct problems before they become more widespread.

Remove Unwanted Growth

Overgrown brush, invasive plants, and unwanted vegetation can compete with desirable plants for sunlight, nutrients, and water. Clearing these areas improves both the appearance and health of your property while making routine maintenance more manageable throughout the growing season.

A well-maintained landscape not only enhances curb appeal but also creates a safer, more enjoyable outdoor environment for family and guests. In most cases, consistency matters more than doing more — steady care through the season protects your investment.

Frequently Asked Questions About Summer Landscaping

Every property has its own challenges, especially during Alabama's warm summer months. The answers below address some of the questions homeowners often have when planning seasonal landscaping improvements and maintaining healthy outdoor spaces.

01 How often should I water my lawn during the summer?

The ideal watering schedule depends on your grass type, soil conditions, and recent rainfall. In general, watering deeply a few times each week is more beneficial than applying small amounts every day. Deep watering encourages stronger root systems, helping grass better withstand periods of heat and limited rainfall. It's also best to water during the early morning when temperatures are lower and evaporation is reduced.

02 Is summer a good time to start a landscaping project?

Yes, many landscaping projects can be completed during the summer with proper planning. Installing mulch, refreshing landscape beds, grading problem areas, adding sod, improving drainage, and preparing sites for future construction are all common warm-weather projects. Some activities may require additional watering while plants become established, so discussing project timing with your contractor can help determine the best approach for your property.

03 How can I improve drainage in my yard?

Standing water often indicates that the property's grading needs attention. Water should naturally flow away from your home and other structures whenever possible. Improving drainage may involve reshaping portions of the yard, correcting low areas, installing drainage solutions, or addressing soil conditions that prevent water from moving efficiently. Evaluating the overall property instead of focusing on one isolated area often leads to better long-term results.

04 What should I do if parts of my yard stay bare?

Bare spots can develop for several reasons, including compacted soil, excessive shade, drainage problems, heavy foot traffic, or poor soil quality. Before reseeding or installing new sod, it's important to identify the underlying cause. Correcting the issue first increases the likelihood that new grass will establish successfully and remain healthy throughout future growing seasons.

05 How do I know when land clearing is necessary?

Land clearing may be appropriate when a property contains heavy brush, invasive vegetation, overgrown tree lines, or areas being prepared for future improvements. Removing unwanted vegetation creates usable space while allowing future grading, landscaping, construction, or drainage work to move forward more efficiently. Every property is different, so the scope of clearing should match the goals of the project.

06 What's the difference between landscaping and grading?

Landscaping generally focuses on improving the appearance and functionality of outdoor spaces through plants, mulch, sod, flower beds, and other enhancements. Grading, on the other hand, involves shaping the land to improve drainage, create level surfaces, or prepare areas for construction. In many projects, both services work together to create an attractive landscape that also performs well over time.

07 How can I keep my landscape looking its best throughout the summer?

Consistency is one of the most effective ways to maintain a healthy landscape. Water appropriately, inspect your property after heavy rainfall, replenish mulch when needed, mow at the recommended height, and address drainage concerns before they become larger problems. Routine maintenance helps protect your investment while keeping your outdoor spaces attractive throughout the season.
